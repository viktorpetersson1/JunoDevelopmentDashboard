The problem

Juno was running on a 27-sheet Excel model

  • 12,815 formulas across portfolio summary, monthly cash flow, debt schedules, capital waterfalls, and risk flags
  • Single-user, version-by-filename — every "what if?" forked a new file
  • No actuals tracking — forecasts lived in Excel, real spending lived in someone's head or a separate file
  • No multi-stakeholder visibility — owners couldn't see the same numbers Viktor was looking at
  • Brittle to change — adding a project meant re-cloning rows across 8 sheets
The spreadsheet already contains the intelligence. The product now needs to make that intelligence understandable, editable, collaborative, and visually elegant.
— Product brief, May 2026
The vision

Atlas runs Juno end-to-end

A daily-driver dashboard for the exec team. Simple enough to look at on a Monday. Trustworthy enough to make capital decisions with.

Risk management

Six categories of risk, every one tied to action

Atlas continuously evaluates the portfolio across six risk categories. Each finding includes severity, financial impact, timing impact, and a suggested mitigation.

Sales delay

Projects past their listing window with no listing date set.

Sale price downside

Margin tested against a 10% market drop.

Cost overrun

Actuals running 5%+ over forecast on any line.

Lender rejection

LTC above 80% — lender may decline.

Equity clustering

Multiple projects need equity simultaneously, exceeding LOC.

Funding gap

Owner equity required above what the LOC can cover.

Decision-grade scenarios

Compare, classify, lock — then own the decision

  • Duplicate any scenario as a starting point for what-ifs
  • Classify as Base · Lender · Upside · Downside · Custom — so anyone reading knows what they're looking at
  • Edit only what changes — the rest inherits from the parent
  • Side-by-side KPI comparison across all saved scenarios — profit, peak equity, max debt, MOIC, IRR
  • Variance drivers — every assumption that's different gets a one-line explanation of why it matters
  • Lock one as the decision — single source of truth, with everything else as alternatives

No more "Base case FINAL v3 (Viktor's edits).xlsx" floating around the team.
